I study how to make machine reasoning *checkable*. That means pairing language models with
formal tools, so what a model produces can be verified instead of trusted, and then using the
verifier's feedback to make the model better.

My current thread applies this to access-control policy synthesis, where a policy that reads
right and a policy that is right can be very different things. The broader question is how
learning and proof can work together, and that question shows up well beyond policies.
